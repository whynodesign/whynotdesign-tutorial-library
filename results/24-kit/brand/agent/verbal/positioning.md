# Positioning

> Fictional test brand. Positioning written by whynotdesign's lab for the kit trial; not client-approved.

**焙曲 / TONEROAST is a small-batch Taiwanese oolong roaster that roasts over charcoal only and writes
down every batch.** Each tin carries one roast, logged like a score: how many rounds over the charcoal,
how long each rest, and a roast level from 1 to 5.

## Who it is for

- **Specialty-coffee drinkers moving into tea.** They already read roast levels, batch numbers and
  roast dates. TONEROAST gives them the same logic.
- **Tea drinkers who like roasted oolong** and are tired of packaging that sells mountains and heritage
  instead of telling them what was done to the leaf.

## What makes it different

1. **Batch-level roast logs** (rounds, rests, roast level 1–5) on every tin.
2. **Charcoal only.** No electric roasting.
3. **The roast itself is the subject.** No leaf, mountain or calligraphy imagery.

## What it is not

Not heritage theatre ("百年傳承"), not luxury (no gold, no "premium"), not wellness (no health claims),
not cute music branding (the score is a log, not a melody).

## Essence

> 焙火如樂譜 · ROAST LIKE A SCORE
> Patient fire, written down.

## When the rules don't cover it

Ask: does this make the roast more legible, or does it decorate it? Choose legible.
